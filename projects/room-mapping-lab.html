Room Mapping Lab

Every supplier names the same room differently. The hard part of room mapping is not matching, it is deciding how much to automate. This lab makes that trade-off concrete on real LiteAPI sandbox data.

The problem

Room mapping makes rates comparable, so travellers see one room with the best price. Each confidence threshold trades automation against wrong rooms booked and review work, and that trade-off is a business decision.

What it does

  • The problem: coverage by city and one hotel up close, with supplier names grouped by master room.
  • Three methods scored against LiteAPI's mapping, with disagreements as an audit list.
  • The decision: threshold, business inputs, net value and sensitivity.
  • An operating plan: metrics, first 90 days, trade-offs for engineering.

Findings and decisions

Confirmed on sandbox data

Simple methods agree on most names, not all

The hybrid picks the same master room as LiteAPI for 85% of 1,710 names. At confidence 0.8 it would auto-map 62% with 94% agreement.

Confirmed, cause is a hypothesis

Whole hotels point to room IDs missing from their room list

In 3 of 187 hotels, all in Barcelona, every rate is mapped to a room ID that is not in the hotel's content room list. It looks like a per-hotel ID mismatch, not a matching error.

Confirmed on sandbox data

Accessible rates mapped to rooms not marked accessible

Of 73 mapped rate names that mention accessibility, 4 are mapped to a room with no accessibility marker, in hotels that have accessible rooms.

Design finding

The best threshold is a business number

With example inputs, halving the cost of a wrong room moves the best threshold from 0.85 to 0.35, doubling it moves it to 0.90.

What I would do next

  1. A per-hotel integrity check for missing room IDs.
  2. An LLM judge for the uncertain slice, with cost and latency measured.
  3. A hand-labelled audit sample to measure accuracy, not agreement.

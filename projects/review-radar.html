Review Radar

Product teams sit on thousands of app reviews spread across stores, countries and languages, and nobody reads them all. Review Radar collects them, has Claude group them into themes with a severity score, and ranks what to fix first, with the original reviews behind every rank.

The question

Can every public review of a product, across all markets and languages, be turned into a ranked and explainable fix list? And how far can the AI part be trusted?

How it works

  1. Collect reviews from the App Store (all storefronts) and Google Play (all major languages).
  2. Claude proposes a list of themes from a balanced sample of reviews.
  3. Claude then tags every review against that fixed list, with a severity score.
  4. A weighted score ranks the themes by frequency, severity, rating impact and recency.

Product calls

Product call

One theme list, then tagging against it

The AI proposes the themes once and then tags every review against the same list. If it invented new themes for each batch, counts would not compare across markets.

Product call

The AI does not choose the order

The ranking is a simple weighted score with adjustable weights (35/25/25/15 by default). A product manager can see why a theme is first and change the weights.

Product call

Every rank links to the original reviews

Each theme shows the reviews behind it, word for word. The AI output is treated as a hypothesis until someone checks it.

Where it stands

Version 0.1.1 has been tested end to end with synthetic data only. The first case study on live reviews is in progress, so there are no findings on this page yet. When it is done, it will include how often the AI's themes and severity scores agree with labels I make by hand.

Limits: Apple's public feed returns only the 500 most recent reviews per country, Google Play data comes from an unofficial library, and people who write reviews skew to the extremes. Free hosting can take a minute to wake up.
